NeuralMesh Pages and Status

RPG NeuralMesh lets a person in one RPG company ask a question and get an answer from what the other companies have written down. Each company keeps its documents on its own node, a server it runs, and the node decides what leaves the company. The centre holds only titles, links and counts.

7company nodes: CEAT, KEC International, Zensar, Harrisons Malayalam, Raychem RPG, RPG Life Sciences and Asvata
9pages, from sign-in to Admin
35knowledge packs at the nodes, the files that describe each company's datapoints and documents
14tools that applications such as SkillForge call through one contract

A typical visit

The order most people use the pages in.

Sign in→Ask NeuralMesh→Expert Finder→Governance, where owners decide→Mesh Nodes and Applications, to see what is connected

The pages

What each page shows, how it helps, and what a person can check there.

Sign in

/autheveryone

What you see

  • Sign-in with email and password. The demo accounts are listed, one per role, with a shared demo password.
  • Sign-up with a company picked from the companies that have a node, and an optional expert profile (job title and skills) that makes the person findable in the Expert Finder.

How it helps

  • Every question and every decision carries the person's company and roles, so each node applies its rules to the right reader.

What you can check

  • The top bar shows who is signed in and for which company.

Dashboard

/everyone

What you see

  • Nodes online (7 of 7), source records copied to the centre (0), companies in your scope and pending approvals.
  • Each company with its level and health, the latest decisions of the nodes, search decisions by node, and how many were allowed and blocked.
  • A value estimate, marked as illustrative and not measured.

How it helps

  • One look tells whether every company is connected and what the nodes decided lately.

What you can check

  • That every node is healthy before a demonstration, and that no source record was copied to the centre.

Ask NeuralMesh

/askeveryoneAI writes the answer, code checks it

What you see

  • A question in your own words, a purpose (problem solving, compliance, bid support, learning or career mobility) and an optional choice of companies.
  • The routing line: how the companies were picked and which topics were found. The graph picks them from the topics of the question, then from the companies the question names, then a model reads the company profiles, then the asker's own company is asked. A person can also choose the companies.
  • The answer, written by the model (Claude Sonnet 5.5) from the evidence the nodes released and nothing else. Each citation shows as the number of its evidence card, and a sentence the evidence does not hold starts with "Not in the evidence:".
  • Approved links between companies, the evidence cards with how far each is shared, the items withheld with a plain reason, and the answer of each node. A summary reached through an approved link has its own card, so every citation points at a card.
  • Grouped figures: when a released item offers a grouped view of its company's own database (CEAT's failure reports by root cause, KEC's delay notes by category, Harrisons Malayalam's spoilage by month and by transport time band, KEC's water and energy figures by year), the node computes the view at that moment and the page shows it as a table card marked "Grouped figures, computed now". Every row covers at least 3 members, smaller groups are dropped at the node, and the answer can cite a row.
  • The answer title shows how long the question took, added up from the steps of the flow.

How it helps

  • A person finds what another company learned, for example how CEAT trends rubber quality per supplier lot for Harrisons Malayalam's latex spoilage, without asking around.
  • Each sentence shows the document it stands on, so the reader can judge it.

What you can check

  • Code checks every answer against seven rules before it is shown: every sentence cites a released record, no test string and no withheld text appears, both sides of a conflict between two documents are shown, no removed instruction, email address or phone number appears, and the answer has at most 10 sentences. When a rule fails, the model writes the answer once more with the rule named, and a second failure is explained in words.
  • When every document that matched is withheld for the chosen purpose, the page says so and suggests asking with another purpose.
  • A document that never leaves its company can show by its title in the withheld list of a question that matched it, with the reason. Its text never leaves.

Expert Finder

/expertseveryone

What you see

  • People who opted in, found by their role, their skills and the topics of their evidence documents, with company and evidence titles. No names.
  • "Request intro" asks the owning company to introduce you.

How it helps

  • Find who in the group has done the work before, with consent on both sides.

What you can check

  • A name shows only to the person who asked, after the owning company approves the introduction for 24 hours.
  • A colleague of your own company is marked "your company". Evidence from a document that never leaves its company is not shown to other companies.

Mesh Nodes

/nodeseveryone

What you see

  • One card per company node: health, level (L1 summaries only, L2 summaries and passages), pack format, datapoints and knowledge packs.
  • "View capabilities": each datapoint and how far it is shared (title only, approved summary, document passages or live lookup), and the node's profile.
  • The mesh graph: an explorer of companies, datapoints, documents, topics, skills, roles and experts with their links, and six graph checks that say what to do when one finds items.

How it helps

  • Shows what each company offers the group and how far, without opening a document.

What you can check

  • A datapoint that never leaves its company is listed only to that company and to mesh admins.

Governance

/governanceeveryoneowner decisions simulated for demo accounts

What you see, in four tabs

Approvals

Requests for access to documents and for expert introductions. The owning company approves for 24 hours or denies. Other companies see "Waiting for" that company.

Links between companies

Links between documents of two companies, for example a CEAT practice that addresses a gap Harrisons Malayalam states. Each company approves its own end, and a link joins answers only after both did.

Application entries

Links an application proposed between its own vocabulary and the companies' data, such as an asset of Snap and Score that belongs to an asset family, grouped by kind. Each side approves its own end.

Decision log

Every decision of every node: time, decision, node, who asked, action, datapoint and the rule in words. No question text, no content, no person. "Check the chains" recomputes the hash chain of all seven ledgers.

How it helps

  • The owner of each company keeps control of what leaves it, and anyone can see that the rules were applied.

What you can check

  • A changed, added or removed entry of a ledger breaks its chain, and "Check the chains" names the node.
  • In this pilot nobody signs in as a company owner: the demo accounts act for the owner roles.

Domains

/domainseveryone

What you see

  • The layers of the graph: the core knowledge layer and the inspection layer of Snap and Score.
  • Each kind of entity with its live count and meaning, each kind of link with its ends, its group and how many are approved and proposed, and the latest load of every source.

How it helps

  • Shows what the graph can hold and what is in it now, so the team behind a new application sees where its data fits.

What you can check

  • References a source could not resolve, and how many links still wait for approval.

Context graph

/context-grapheveryone, uploads by the mesh team

What you see

  • Uploaded inputs: the context graphs, each a named set of files a mesh admin added, with a search box over every word of an input (title, file, company, item, kind, tags, first sentences, graph name). A graph shows its inputs, the chosen input's record with what leaves the company in words ("Summary leaves CEAT", "Shared with every connected company"), and the part of the graph all its inputs made, drawn together with each concept once and how it matched (named in the text, all its words in the text, from a tag).
  • Add input: a file (.txt, .md, .csv, .json, .pdf, .docx) for Others, the inputs that belong to no one company and the default choice, or for one of the seven companies and one of its catalogue items, into a new context graph with a name or into an existing one, with the item's sharing rule shown before the file is sent.
  • Search the graph: a word, phrase, tag or catalogue code, the matching entities with their type, company, sharing rule and link count, and one entity with its links by type.

How it helps

  • Knowledge gets into the mesh from a file, not only from the built packs, by the same path: the company's node, or the shared node for Others, indexes it under the item's rule, and the central graph keeps its title and links only.
  • Inputs on one topic build up one context graph: the second input meets the first at the concepts they share, and the whole graph can be downloaded as JSON, GraphML, CSV or a knowledge pack that carries, for each document, what its item releases. "Ask about this" turns an entity into a question on the Ask page.

What you can check

  • Whether an upload reached the graph and what it was linked to, that a document under a never-leave item is shown to its own company and the mesh team only, and that a shared input is listed to every company. A mesh admin removes one input or drops a whole graph, and the records stay marked removed.

Applications

/applicationseveryone

What you see

  • The applications connected to NeuralMesh, SkillForge and Snap and Score, from their registry files: the identity they use, the tools they call and what they loaded.
  • For SkillForge, the packs it sends: how many are kept, when one last loaded, the latest send (loaded, unchanged, refused) and the reason of any pack refused and not loaded since.

How it helps

  • One place to see that an application is connected, that its data arrived and why a pack was refused.

What you can check

  • An application reads evidence only through the same tools as a person and never holds another company's documents.

Admin

/adminfor the mesh team

What you see

  • The registered node endpoints, the model used for each step (classify, re-rank, answer), the guardrails, and the shared vocabulary: topics with their other names.

How it helps

  • The mesh team sees which model writes answers, which nodes are registered and which names lead to which topic.

What you can check

  • Outside development, strict mode refuses a cloud model and no Confidential record goes to a cloud model. In this development pilot synthetic Confidential records may, because every pack is synthetic.

Decisions for the product owner

Working as built, but the intended behaviour is not settled.

  1. Should single words such as "latex" lead to a topic, or should the vocabulary hold phrases only?
  2. Should the aggregate views of O-05, E-02 and O-06 come back as evidence?
  3. Should the Expert Finder card show the expert's ID?
  4. Should the name of a colleague of the asker's own company show without an introduction?
  5. A person of another company sees the title of a never-leave document that matched a question, marked as withheld. Is that intended?
  6. An answer routed by the graph lists the approved links of its documents, which can name a company that was not asked. Is that intended?

Words used

The terms of the pages, each in one line.

NodeA company's own server. It keeps the company's documents and decides what leaves.
Knowledge packA file that lists a company's datapoints, documents and their links for one domain.
DatapointA group of documents with one owner and one sharing rule, for example E-03 materials handling learnings.
Never-leave datapointA datapoint whose documents never leave the company, for example CEAT's compound formulations.
PurposeWhy a question is asked. A datapoint lists the purposes it is shared for.
Evidence recordA summary or passage a node released, with its citation. It is the only input the model writes from.
RoutingPicking the companies a question goes to.
TopicA concept of the shared vocabulary, such as incoming quality control, with its other names.
WithheldA document that matched but that the node did not release, shown by its title with the reason.
Approved linkA link between documents of two companies that both owners approved.
Decision ledgerThe log of a node's decisions, chained by hash so a change shows.
Context contractThe one request and answer format every application uses, version 1.1, with 14 tools.
Talent packSkillForge's skill list or one company's roles and required skills, sent to NeuralMesh.
Mesh adminA person who runs the mesh, such as the presenter account. Sees every company's never-leave datapoints by title.
