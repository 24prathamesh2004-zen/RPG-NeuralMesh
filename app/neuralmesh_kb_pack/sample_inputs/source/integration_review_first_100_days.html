Integration review, first 100 days of the overseas unit

Review by the project management office of KEC International, July 2026.

The purchase and invoice systems of the acquired unit were cut over on day 21, before the process owners were agreed. Site teams rebuilt their old steps as 14 workarounds outside the new system. Local labour, contract and payment terms were first reviewed on day 63. That review took more time than was planned at close.

What happened in the first 100 days

DayEvent
Day 0Close of the acquisition. One integration lead was named. No process owner was named.
Day 21System cut-over for purchase orders and supplier invoices.
Day 21 to day 45Site teams kept spreadsheets beside the new system for purchase requests, goods receipts and subcontractor bills. The count on day 45 was 14 workarounds.
Day 63First review of local labour rules, contract terms and payment cycles. Subcontractors on site were paid every two weeks, and the group system paid once a month.
Day 80Process owners were named for purchase to payment, site payroll and subcontractor billing.
Day 1009 of the 14 workarounds were closed. The other 5 each have an owner and a closing date.

Why it went this way

  • The cut-over date was fixed in the plan at close and was not tied to a sign-off.
  • Process ownership was treated as a task for the weeks after the cut-over.
  • The review of local labour, contract and payment terms had no date in the plan.

What the next integration should do

  1. Name the owner of each core process before day 0.
  2. Start a system cut-over only after the process owner signs off the target process.
  3. Review local labour, contract and payment terms by day 30.
  4. Keep one list of workarounds, each with an owner and a closing date, and read it every week.
