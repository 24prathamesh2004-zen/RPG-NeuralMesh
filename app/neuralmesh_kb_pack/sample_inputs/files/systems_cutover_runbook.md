# Systems cut-over runbook for an acquired unit

This runbook is the cut-over part of the integration playbook. It is used when the systems of an acquired unit move to the systems of the buyer. A cut-over starts only after the process owner signs off the target process. Both toolchains stay in use until that sign-off, and the old tools are retired in phases.

## Before the cut-over

- Name one integration lead and the owner of each core process before day 0.
- Write the target process with the process owner, and run it on paper with the site teams for two weeks.
- List every spreadsheet and manual step that the site teams use today, with the name of the team that uses it.
- Review local labour, contract and payment terms by day 30, and change the target process where a local rule needs it.
- Agree one communication plan for staff and one for customers.
- Take a full copy of the old system and test that it can be read.

## On the day

1. The integration lead confirms that the process owner has signed the sign-off sheet.
2. The old system is set to read only at 18:00 local time.
3. Open purchase orders, open invoices and open payroll items are moved and counted. The counts of the old and the new system must be equal.
4. The process owner and one member of each site team run 10 real transactions in the new system.
5. If a count differs or a transaction is rejected, the old system is opened again and the cut-over moves by one week.

## After the cut-over

- For 30 days the integration lead holds a 15 minute call with the site teams on every working day.
- Every workaround that a team reports goes into one list, with an owner and a closing date.
- The old tools are retired in phases. A tool is switched off after 30 days without a transaction.
- On day 30 the process owner reads the list of workarounds and decides which ones change the target process.

## Sign-off

The cut-over is closed when the process owner, the integration lead and the head of finance of the unit sign three points: the counts match, payroll and supplier payments have run once in the new system, and no workaround is open without an owner.
